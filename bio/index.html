The Guy Behind the Work

Welcome to the Workshop.

I'm Dave Kangas. There's usually something on the bench around here, so come on in and have a look.

A Little About Me

I build things.

For a good portion of my life, that meant working with steel, welding equipment, and an oxyacetylene torch. These days, it might mean developing an iPhone app, designing a business management system, or figuring out whether an outrageous engine swap would actually work.

Different tools. Same curiosity.

The Long Way Around

I started my professional life as a boilermaker. It wasn't exactly a childhood dream. I went to welding school because I needed to figure out what I was going to do with myself, and an instructor suggested I might make a decent boilermaker.

Turns out, Dan knew what he was talking about.

I worked my way from apprentice to general foreman in industrial construction. I loved the work, especially the challenges that required a little ingenuity and the occasional creative use of language.

Unfortunately, my heart had other ideas.

Heart failure brought my career in the trades to an early end and sent me down a few roads I hadn't anticipated. I went back to school, earned a business degree, and spent some time figuring out what the next chapter was supposed to look like.

Eventually, I moved to Florida, underwent a heart transplant, and decided that maybe I wasn't finished reinventing myself.

So I went back to school again.

This time I earned a Juris Master in Legal Risk Management from Florida State University. Somewhere along the way, I discovered that modern software tools could help me turn some of the ideas rattling around in my head into actual working products.

It's been a winding road, but every turn seems to have added another tool to the box.

And I've always liked tools.

A Different Kind of Workshop

My interest in software began with a familiar frustration: I couldn't find exactly what I wanted.

I have a particular dislike for products that are almost right. The ones that make you jump through six screens to accomplish something that ought to take two taps.

So I started building my own.

My first published app, ANVAIR, is an Apple Watch workout timer designed to do one thing exceptionally well. It looks pretty simple, which is exactly the point. Getting it to feel that simple took considerably more work than I expected.

That's become something of a philosophy around here.

I like focused tools. Your phone is already a Swiss Army knife. I don't need to build another one. I'd rather make a really good attachment for the one you've already got.

Sometimes that means writing software. Other times it means using tools like AppSheet to turn a collection of notebooks, checklists, and half-remembered conversations into an actual business management system.

The technology is interesting, but making something useful is the fun part.

More Than Software

Kangas Works is one part of a larger collection of creative and entrepreneurial pursuits.

My wife, Jody, and I operate Heart & Harbor, a lifestyle apparel brand featuring our original artwork and designs. It's a way to get our creative work out into the world without having to explain it to somebody in an art gallery.

I also operate Homeward Support, a property monitoring business here on Florida's Gulf Coast. What started as helping neighbors keep an eye on their homes has grown into a proper business, complete with a management and reporting system I built myself.

And then there are the projects that exist simply because I think they'd be fun.

I've always had a fascination with cars and boats. I can spend an unreasonable amount of time contemplating the engineering behind a completely impractical automotive creation.

Sometimes the problem you're solving is how to make a workout more efficient.

Sometimes it's figuring out what a '71 Chevelle with an LS7 and 4.11 gears might run in the quarter mile.

Both seem like perfectly reasonable uses of an afternoon to me.

Kangas Works

The name has a couple of meanings.

There's the connection to the workshops, fabrication shops, and industrial environments where I spent my early career.

But there's also a little wordplay.

Kangas? He works.

I like that.

I'm not entirely sure whether Kangas Works is a software studio, a creative workshop disguised as a business, or a business disguised as a creative workshop.

Maybe I'll have a better answer in five years.

What I do know is that I'm enjoying this chapter of my life. I've had some extraordinary experiences, a few I'd rather not repeat, and plenty of opportunities to discover what I'm capable of.

I don't feel much need to prove anything anymore.

There's always another project on the bench, another idea worth exploring, and something new to learn. I don't expect that will ever change.

But I've reached a point where I can look at the things I've done, the people in my life, and the things I'm still creating and feel genuinely satisfied.

I'm not finished. Not even close.

But I've done enough to be happy with where I am.

Anyway, that's enough about me. What are you working on? Drop me a line. And if you want to talk boats, you might want to pull up a chair.
